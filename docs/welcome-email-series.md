# The First 72 Hours: 5-day welcome series

Five short emails for people who ask for the checklist at `/first-72-hours` (and,
optionally, everyone who joins the list). Each one has a single link to a page that
already exists on partywreckers.com, and everything in them comes from Matt's own
articles. Paste them into Mailchimp as written; the bracketed bits are notes, not copy.

Timing: **day 0, 1, 2, 4 and 6** after the subscriber confirms.

---

## Email 1 (day 0): Your checklist, and why the first 72 hours matter

**Subject:** Your checklist: The First 72 Hours
**Preview text:** Keep it somewhere you'll find it on a bad night.

Hi,

Thanks for asking for the checklist. Here it is again, so it's in one easy place:

**[The First 72 Hours: a family's checklist after an addiction crisis](https://partywreckers.com/first-72-hours)**

Print it or save it as a PDF, and put it somewhere you'll find it at two in the morning.

I've sat in a lot of hospital hallways at four in the morning. Fluorescent lights, vending machine coffee, a family sitting three feet apart and not talking. The question always comes out the same way: "So what do we do now?"

Here's the honest answer. After an overdose, an arrest, a DUI or an ER visit, there's usually a short window, a day or three, when the person you love is scared enough to say yes to help they'd normally refuse. It's driven by fear and exhaustion, not insight. That's fine. Willingness doesn't have to be pure to be useful.

The window closes fast, because shame comes back once the fear fades. The families who use it well aren't luckier or smarter. They're the ones who already had the plan and the phone number in somebody's pocket.

That's what this checklist is for, and what the next few emails are about. Short, practical, one thing at a time.

If anyone is in danger right now, don't read further. Call 911, or call or text 988.

Matt

## Email 2 (day 1): The plan nobody wants to make

**Subject:** The hour that keeps someone alive
**Preview text:** It isn't giving up. It's a seatbelt.

Hi,

I got sober on April 6, 2003. What still stops me cold isn't how close I came to losing everything. It's how close I came to not being here at all. One night a friend rolled me onto my side and sat with me until morning. He had no training and no plan. He just didn't leave.

Most families I meet have spent months researching treatment centers and zero minutes preparing for the Tuesday night that goes wrong. Making a safety plan feels like admitting the worst is possible. You already know it is.

It takes about an hour, and it doesn't need your loved one's permission:

- Naloxone (Narcan), at least two doses, where anyone in the house can reach it. It's over the counter and won't hurt someone who isn't overdosing.
- A written list of what they use, their prescriptions and medical conditions, taped inside a cabinet door.
- Everyone in the house, teenagers included, knowing where it all is.

And no, this isn't enabling. Enabling protects someone from consequences. Naloxone protects them from dying, which isn't a consequence anyone learns from.

Here's the whole plan, step by step, including exactly what to do if you can't wake them:

**[The Overdose Safety Plan Every Family Needs](https://partywreckers.com/blog/overdose-safety-plan-for-families)**

The safety plan isn't the strategy. It's the seatbelt you wear on the way to the strategy.

Matt

## Email 3 (day 2): You don't need a plan. You need a pen.

**Subject:** The green spiral notebook
**Preview text:** Five minutes tonight. It changes the first phone call.

Hi,

A few years ago a mother slid a green spiral notebook across her kitchen table to me. "I don't know if this helps," she said. "I just started writing things down."

Fourteen months of entries. A missed Thanksgiving. Four hundred dollars gone from her purse. A 2 a.m. call where her son was slurring so badly she couldn't understand him. It's still one of the most useful things a family has ever handed me. Not because it was dramatic. Because it was true.

Here's why it matters. Memory bends toward hope, and one good week can quietly erase a terrible month. Addiction bends the truth: "It was one time." "You're exaggerating." I said all of those, with total confidence. A dated entry doesn't get into a debate. It just sits there being accurate.

Keep it simple: the date, what you saw or heard, what it cost, and how it affected you. "Oct 12, 9:40 p.m. Came home two hours late, said traffic. Couldn't finish sentences. Our daughter asked if Dad was sick." That's a whole entry.

Keep it private, never read it aloud in a fight, write the good days too, and cap it at five minutes.

**[Why documenting a loved one's addiction is the most underrated thing you can do tonight](https://partywreckers.com/blog/documenting-a-loved-ones-addiction)**

Her son went to treatment. Later he told me, "I could argue with how she felt. I couldn't argue with the dates."

Matt

## Email 4 (day 4): The cleanup you do on autopilot

**Subject:** Most enabling starts as love
**Preview text:** Pick one thing and don't do it this time.

Hi,

Most enabling starts as love. You help because you're scared they'll get hurt, because you're keeping the peace, because you remember who they were before. That isn't being foolish. It's what people do when they're desperate for the suffering to stop.

The problem is that a lot of that help quietly protects the addiction from its consequences. Calling the boss to say they're sick. Paying for the tow or the ticket. Apologizing to the neighbors. Telling the kids "Dad's just tired."

Here's the simple test: does this reduce today's discomfort but keep the pattern going? Or does it support their health while letting reality be real?

You don't have to overhaul your whole house this week. Pick one piece of cleanup and don't do it this time. Then write down one boundary in three parts: the line, what *you* will do, and the support you'll still offer. For example: "If you come home intoxicated, you can't stay here that night. I'll still drive you to an evaluation in the morning."

Say it calmly, say it once, and expect pushback. Pushback doesn't mean the boundary is wrong. It often means it's working.

**[Enabling vs Supporting: a boundary blueprint for families](https://partywreckers.com/blog/enabling-vs-supporting-boundaries)**

Boundaries aren't cruelty. They're clarity, and clarity is often the first step toward change.

Matt

## Email 5 (day 6): The call you keep not making

**Subject:** Eleven months with my number in her phone
**Preview text:** The first call is a conversation, not a contract.

Hi,

A woman once told me she had my number saved in her phone for eleven months before she dialed it. She'd pull it up in the grocery store parking lot, look at it, and put the phone face down on the passenger seat. She wasn't stalling, she said. She was waiting for the right moment.

That moment doesn't exist. Making the call makes it real, and underneath that is shame: the feeling that a loved one's addiction proves you got something wrong. You didn't cause this. You also can't love someone out of it by trying harder in private.

The first call isn't a sales pitch, and it isn't the intervention. It's usually 20 to 45 minutes. You talk, I listen and ask questions, and you hang up with a clearer picture, whether or not you ever hire anybody. You don't need the whole family on board to make it.

If you can jot down a rough timeline, what you're paying for each month, who could be in the room, and any medical or treatment history, the call goes twice as far. Your first sentence can be as simple as: "I don't know what to do next."

**[The Phone Call You Keep Not Making](https://partywreckers.com/blog/the-phone-call-you-keep-not-making)**

Her son has almost four years now. The eleven months are over. She made the call. That's the part that counts.

Matt

## Mailchimp setup

The site already does the tagging: every checklist signup reaches Mailchimp as a
**pending** member (double opt-in) tagged `partywreckers.com`, `source:lead_72_hours`
and **`lead:72-hours`**. Quiz signups are tagged `source:quiz_<result>` and
`quiz:<result>` (`quiz:watching`, `quiz:stuck`, `quiz:ready`, `quiz:crisis`).

1. **Create the journey.** Automations → Customer Journeys → *Create journey* → *Build
   from scratch*. Name it "First 72 Hours welcome" and pick the audience set in
   `MAILCHIMP_AUDIENCE_ID`.
2. **Starting point.** Choose **Tag added** → tag **`lead:72-hours`**.
   - Journeys only email *subscribed* contacts, and new signups are pending until they
     click Mailchimp's confirmation email. Send yourself a test signup (step 7). If the
     contact doesn't enter the journey after confirming, switch the starting point to
     **Signs up** (fires when a double opt-in contact confirms) and add an **If/Else**
     rule right after it: *Tags → contact is tagged → `lead:72-hours`*. Put the emails
     on the "yes" branch; leave the "no" branch empty (or use it for a general welcome).
   - To send the series to *every* new subscriber instead, use **Signs up** with no
     If/Else.
   - In the starting point's settings, allow contacts to enter **only once**.
3. **Email 1 (day 0):** add *Send email* directly after the starting point. Paste the
   subject, preview text and body from Email 1 above. Mailchimp adds the unsubscribe
   footer and your physical address automatically.
4. **Email 2 (day 1):** add *Time delay* → 1 day, then *Send email* (Email 2).
5. **Email 3 (day 2):** *Time delay* → 1 day, then Email 3.
6. **Email 4 (day 4):** *Time delay* → 2 days, then Email 4. **Email 5 (day 6):**
   *Time delay* → 2 days, then Email 5.
   - Optional: in each delay, choose "Send on specific days/times" and pick a morning
     time in your audience's time zone.
7. **Test.** Sign up at `https://partywreckers.com/first-72-hours` with an address you
   own, confirm the Mailchimp email, and check the contact's profile shows the
   `lead:72-hours` tag and that it entered the journey. Then **Turn on** the journey.

Notes:

- Email 1 repeats the checklist link on purpose. The site's own one-off email (sent
  through Resend) only goes out when `AUTO_REPLY_FROM` is set and Turnstile verified
  the visitor; see `docs/new-features-setup.md`. Email 1 makes sure everyone gets it.
- Someone who is already on the list ("Member Exists" in Mailchimp) isn't re-tagged by
  the site, so they won't enter a tag-triggered journey. They still get the Resend
  checklist email if that's configured. Add the tag by hand if you want them in it.
- Quiz takers can get a follow-up too: build a second journey on **Tag added** →
  `quiz:stuck` (and so on), or add those tags as extra If/Else branches.
- All links point at pages that exist today. If an article's slug ever changes, update
  the link here and in Mailchimp.
